Two ways to move the same data

Different physics, same goal: getting bits from one device to another.

Ethernet / Copper

Ethernet cables use copper wires and electrical signals to connect devices like PCs, routers, switches, consoles, and Wi-Fi access points.

Fiber Optic

Fiber-optic cables use tiny strands of glass or plastic and flashes of light to move data.

Copper is like cars on a road

Signals travel as electrical current, sharing the "road" with resistance and interference along the way.

Fiber is like light through a tunnel

Signals travel as flashes of light bouncing down a glass tunnel — fast, and largely undisturbed by the outside world.

Cat6a

Common use: future-ready offices, high-speed installs.

10 Gbps up to 100 m.

Multimode fiber

Common use: shorter high-speed runs inside buildings and data centers.

Single-mode fiber

Common use: long links between buildings, cities, or sites.

Which should I choose?

Match the cable to the job in front of you — scroll through each scenario.

Home setup

Choose Cat6 or Cat6a for most wired home devices.

Gaming & streaming

Copper Ethernet is ideal for a console, PC, router, or switch in the same room or house.

Connecting buildings

Choose fiber when the run needs to beat normal copper distance limits, or cross between buildings.

School, office, data center

Fiber for backbone and high-capacity uplinks; copper for desks, access points, phones, nearby devices.
